Weighted Average MarketsSample story · Jio Platforms IPO

Jio IPO Closes at 22.19x; QIB Portion at 70x — Listing Tomorrow at ₹1,344 GMP

What happened. Jio Platforms' ₹30,213 crore IPO closed on October 23 with final subscriptions of 22.19 times overall. The QIB (qualified institutional buyer) tranche was subscribed 70.55 times, the non-institutional investor (NII or HNI) portion closed at 14 times, and the retail individual investor portion was 2.72 times subscribed. The grey market premium heading into listing stands at approximately ₹225, implying an indicative listing price of ₹1,344 against the ₹1,119 upper band, a potential 20% premium. Jio Platforms lists on the BSE and NSE tomorrow, October 28.

The second order. A 70.55x QIB subscription is exceptional even by Indian IPO standards. It means institutional investors collectively bid for 70 times the shares available to them, a level of conviction that suggests funds are not just trading the listing pop but building positions for the long-term story. The retail subscription of 2.72 times is telling in its own way: retail came in, but not in the frenzy that QIBs showed.

For allotment, retail applicants who applied for one lot of 12 shares at ₹1,119 (₹13,428 outlay) have a roughly 1-in-3 chance of getting shares, given the 2.72x retail subscription. NII applicants are allotted by lottery at the minimum application size, so roughly 1 in 14 will get shares. The listing day dynamic will be set by retail and NII holders who got allotment and are deciding whether to sell into the opening pop or hold. With anchor investors locked in, the float on October 28 is limited, which tends to support the GMP if demand holds.

The QuestionQIBs subscribed 70 times and retail only 2.72 times. Should a retail investor be worried that institutions know something retail doesn't, or relieved that they can actually get allotment?
The AnswerBoth readings have merit, but they apply to different time horizons. The 70x QIB subscription tells you that the large, well-resourced investors with access to management meetings and detailed financial models are strongly backing the Jio story over three to five years. The 2.72x retail subscription tells you that the average Indian investor is not applying in panic mode; they are measured. For allotment, 2.72x retail actually works in your favour: the lottery odds are reasonable, roughly one in three applications gets shares, versus the nightmare allotment ratios in the 30-50x retail subscriptions you sometimes see in hot SME IPOs. For tomorrow's listing, the GMP of ₹225 is pricing in a 20% pop. If you got allotment and your primary interest is the listing gain, that is a reasonable outcome to watch for. If your interest is the long-term Jio story (ARPU growth, 5G monetisation, digital services), then the listing price is largely irrelevant: you are buying a business, not a day-one trade.
